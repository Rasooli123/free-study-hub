Chapter 8: Natural Numbers, Divisibility & Fundamental Arithmetic

Practice questions based on the chapter.

1. Which of the following numbers is divisible by 3?

A number is divisible by 3 when the sum of its digits is divisible by 3. For 348, 3 + 4 + 8 = 15, and 15 is divisible by 3.

2. Which number is divisible by 9?

729 has digit sum 7 + 2 + 9 = 18. Since 18 is divisible by 9, 729 is divisible by 9.

3. What is the greatest common divisor of 24 and 36?

The common divisors of 24 and 36 include 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12. Therefore, the greatest common divisor is 12.

4. What is the least common multiple of 8 and 12?

Multiples of 8 include 8, 16, 24, ... Multiples of 12 include 12, 24, ... Therefore, the least common multiple is 24.

5. If a number is divisible by both 4 and 6, it must be divisible by which number?

A number divisible by both 4 and 6 must be divisible by their least common multiple. LCM(4, 6) = 12.

6. Which of the following is a prime number?

29 has only two positive divisors: 1 and 29. Therefore, 29 is a prime number.

7. How many positive divisors does 12 have?

The positive divisors of 12 are: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12. Therefore, there are 6 positive divisors.

8. What is the remainder when 47 is divided by 6?

47 = 6 × 7 + 5. Therefore, the remainder is 5.

9. If n leaves remainder 3 when divided by 5, what is the remainder of n + 7 when divided by 5?

n has remainder 3 and 7 has remainder 2 when divided by 5. Therefore, the total remainder is 3 + 2 = 5, which gives a remainder of 0.

10. Which digit can replace * in 52* so that the number is divisible by 3?

The digit sum is 5 + 2 + * = 7 + *. For divisibility by 3, the digit sum must be divisible by 3. If * = 2, the sum is 9. Therefore, 522 is divisible by 3.

11. Which digit should replace * in 4*8 so that the number is divisible by 9?

For divisibility by 9: 4 + * + 8 must be divisible by 9. Therefore, 12 + * = 18, giving * = 6.

12. What is the greatest two-digit number divisible by 7?

7 × 14 = 98. The next multiple is 105, which has three digits. Therefore, 98 is the greatest two-digit multiple of 7.

13. What is the smallest positive integer divisible by 3, 4 and 5?

We need LCM(3, 4, 5). Since these numbers have no common prime factor, LCM = 3 × 4 × 5 = 60.

14. If gcd(a, b) = 6 and lcm(a, b) = 60, which could be a possible pair (a, b)?

gcd(12, 30) = 6 and lcm(12, 30) = 60. Therefore, (12, 30) is a valid pair.

15. How many positive divisors does 36 have?

36 = 2² × 3². The number of positive divisors is: (2 + 1)(2 + 1) = 9.

16. What is the sum of the first 10 natural numbers?

The first 10 positive natural numbers are 1 through 10. Their sum is: 10 × 11 ÷ 2 = 55.

17. What is the sum of all even numbers from 2 to 20?

There are 10 even numbers from 2 to 20. Their average is (2 + 20) ÷ 2 = 11. Therefore: 10 × 11 = 110.

18. If n is an odd integer, which expression is always even?

An odd integer plus 1 is always even. Therefore, n + 1 is always even.

19. If a number is divisible by 2 and 3, which statement must be true?

Since 2 and 3 have no common factor other than 1, a number divisible by both must be divisible by 2 × 3 = 6.

20. What is the remainder when 2³⁵ is divided by 2?

Any positive power of 2 is divisible by 2. Therefore, the remainder is 0.

21. If n is divisible by 8, which of the following is always divisible by 4?

If n is divisible by 8, then n is also divisible by 4. Since 4 is also divisible by 4, n + 4 must be divisible by 4.

22. What is the greatest common divisor of 48, 72 and 120?

gcd(48, 72) = 24. Since 120 is also divisible by 24, the greatest common divisor of all three numbers is 24.

23. What is the smallest positive integer that leaves remainder 2 when divided by both 5 and 7?

We need n − 2 to be divisible by both 5 and 7. The least common multiple of 5 and 7 is 35. Therefore: n = 35 + 2 = 37.

24. A number has prime factorization 2³ × 3² × 5. How many positive divisors does it have?

For 2³ × 3² × 5¹, the number of positive divisors is: (3 + 1)(2 + 1)(1 + 1) = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24.

25. Which statement is always true for two consecutive natural numbers n and n + 1?

Two consecutive integers always contain one even number. Therefore, their product is always even.
