MATHEMATICS 2

Permutation–Combination & Binomial–Probability

Chapter 15 · 25 Questions

1. What is 5!?

5! = 5·4·3·2·1 = 120.

2. How many permutations are there of 5 objects taken 2 at a time?

P(5, 2) = 5·4 = 20.

3. How many combinations are there of 5 objects taken 2 at a time?

C(5, 2) = 5!/(2!3!) = 10.

4. How many ways can 3 students be chosen from 8 students?

Order does not matter: C(8, 3) = 8·7·6/(3·2·1) = 56.

5. How many arrangements can be made from 6 objects taken 3 at a time?

P(6, 3) = 6·5·4 = 120.

6. How many different arrangements of the letters in CAT are possible?

All three letters are different, so there are 3! = 6 arrangements.

7. How many ways can 4 people stand in a line?

The number of line arrangements is 4! = 24.

8. How many committees of 2 can be selected from 6 people?

C(6, 2) = 6·5/2 = 15.

9. How many distinct arrangements of the letters in LEVEL are possible?

There are 5 letters with two Ls and two Es: 5!/(2!2!) = 30.

10. How many ways can 4 people sit around a circular table?

Circular arrangements of n distinct people are (n − 1)!, so 3! = 6.

11. What is the coefficient of x³ in (x + 1)⁵?

The x³ term has coefficient C(5, 3) = 10.

12. What is the coefficient of x² in (2x + 1)⁴?

Choose two factors of 2x: C(4, 2)(2x)² = 6·4x² = 24x².

13. What is the probability of rolling an even number on a fair six-sided die?

The even outcomes are 2, 4, and 6: 3 out of 6, or 1/2.

14. What is the probability of getting two heads when two fair coins are tossed?

The equally likely outcomes are HH, HT, TH, TT. Only HH gives two heads.

15. A bag has 3 red and 2 blue balls. What is the probability of drawing a red ball?

There are 3 red balls among 5 total, so the probability is 3/5.

16. What is the probability that the sum is 7 when two fair dice are rolled?

There are six favorable pairs: (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1), out of 36. Thus 6/36 = 1/6.

17. What is the probability of getting at least one head when two fair coins are tossed?

All outcomes except TT have at least one head, so 3 out of 4 outcomes are favorable.

18. What is 0!?

By definition, 0! = 1.

19. Find P(7, 3).

P(7, 3) = 7·6·5 = 210.

20. What is C(10, 0)?

There is exactly one way to choose no objects: C(10, 0) = 1.

21. How many binary strings of length 4 are there?

Each of the 4 positions has 2 choices, so 2⁴ = 16 strings.

22. From 8 people, how many ways can a president and a secretary be chosen?

The two roles are different, so order matters: 8·7 = 56.

23. What is the probability of drawing an ace from a standard 52-card deck?

There are 4 aces in 52 cards, so 4/52 = 1/13.

24. What is the coefficient of x³ in (x + 2)⁵?

Choose three x factors: C(5, 3)x³·2² = 10·4x³ = 40x³.

25. How many ways can 3 different books be arranged on a shelf?

The books are distinct, so there are 3! = 6 arrangements.
